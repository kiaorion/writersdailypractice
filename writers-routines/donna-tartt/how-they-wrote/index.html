How Long Does Donna Tartt Take to Write a Novel?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Donna Tartt publishes about once a decade, and the slow pace is deliberate. Each of her three novels took years of longhand drafting before it reached a shelf. This is what that timeline actually looks like.

The Direct Answer
About ten years per novel, and she's held that pace across all three books.
The Secret History (1992), The Little Friend (2002), and The Goldfinch (2013) each arrived roughly a decade apart, a rhythm she's talked about in her Queen's Reading Room interview.

Three Books, Three Decades

Tartt's output is famously slow. The Secret History came out in 1992, The Little Friend in 2002, and The Goldfinch in 2013. That's roughly one novel every ten or eleven years, and she's never tried to close the gap.

The books themselves ate up most of that time. The Secret History is often reported to have taken around eight years, tracing back to her student days at Bennington College, where a classmate, the writer Bret Easton Ellis, helped bring the manuscript to the attention of a literary agent. When it finally landed in 1992 it was a critical and commercial hit. The Little Friend and The Goldfinch each ran to roughly a decade of work after that.

Exact start dates are fuzzy, because she stays quiet while a book is in progress, so treat the year counts as close estimates rather than stopwatch figures. What's clear is the shape: long gaps, each filled with steady, unglamorous work.

A Decade of Small Days

The ten years aren't one heroic marathon. Tartt describes herself as a slow writer who writes all the time, and her days are built around a short, protected morning session of roughly three hours. When the writing is going well she keeps going; when it isn't, she stops and does something else rather than force bad pages.

She builds the work at the level of the individual sentence, crafting a line until it sits exactly right before she moves on. Stack enough of those mornings together and you get a decade, and eventually a novel.

Why She Won't Speed Up

The slowness is a choice. Tartt has said that being pushed to work faster just makes her unhappy, and she'd rather get one book right than push out several she doesn't believe in. That patience shapes everything from her research to the way she builds a single sentence.

All my novels, including the one I'm writing now, are very different from each other, and to invent new worlds and new characters from the ground up takes a while.

Donna Tartt, The Queen's Reading Room

What You Can Steal

  • Let the work set the deadline. A decade is fine when the decade earns it.
  • Keep the project off the table until it's done. Tartt won't discuss a novel in progress, which keeps the pressure internal.
  • Choose a pace you can hold for years. Hers is slow and daily, and it compounds.
  • Let ambition set the scope. Every book of hers tries something the last one didn't.
