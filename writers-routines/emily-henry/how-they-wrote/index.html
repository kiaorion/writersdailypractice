How Does Emily Henry Write a Book a Year?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Six adult romances in six years, from Beach Read in 2020 to Great Big Beautiful Life in 2025. Henry doesn't reach that pace by writing fast, clean prose. She writes fast, messy prose, then spends far longer turning it into a book.

The Direct Answer
She front-loads a quick, deliberately rough first draft, then spends months revising it into a book.
Her Beach Read draft took about three to five weeks; the revision took far longer, per The London Magazine and Writer's Digest.

The Fast, Bad First Draft

Henry's cadence starts with speed. She's said the first draft of Beach Read took somewhere between three and five weeks, written quickly and badly on purpose (The London Magazine). She lets a ball of momentum roll downhill and carry her through, and she doesn't stop to fix things along the way. A finished bad draft is something she can work with. A perfect first chapter with no book behind it isn't.

I am not afraid to write a terrible first draft; in fact it's very comforting for me to finish a draft no matter how bad it is.

Emily Henry, The London Magazine

That comfort with a bad draft is the engine of the whole thing. Henry plans very little going in and discovers the story as she writes, so the quick draft doubles as her outline. She'll even bang out a fast test draft of a new idea just to see whether it feels as good on the page as it did in her head before she commits to the full book. Getting words down fast is how she finds out what she's actually got.

Then Months of Revision, One Book a Year

The draft is the fast part. The book gets made in revision, and that takes far longer. For People We Meet on Vacation, Henry spent roughly six months reworking her draft on her own before she handed it to her editor (Writer's Digest). She's described a fresh draft as mostly dialogue and scraps of description, not yet a book, which is exactly what those months of rewriting are for. Each pass gets a little closer to the version readers eventually hold.

The result is a steady clip: a new adult romance nearly every year since Beach Read in 2020, running through Great Big Beautiful Life in 2025. Henry is candid that the yearly schedule brings real pressure, since once a book a year becomes the expectation, a writer can hit the deadline without feeling they've lived enough to have something new to say. Keeping the drafting quick is how she protects the long revision window that does the real work.

See how it fits her wider habits in the full Emily Henry writing routine.

What You Can Steal

  • Separate drafting from editing. Write fast first and judge later, so the two jobs stop stepping on each other.
  • Aim to finish, not to impress. A complete rough draft beats a flawless fragment every time.
  • Budget most of your time for revision. Her draft took weeks; the rewrite took months.
  • Use a test draft. A quick pass tells you whether an idea is worth the full build.
