How Many Words Is The Great Gatsby?

Kia Orion | August 2, 2026 | 5 min read

The Great Gatsby is short. Roughly 47,000 to 48,000 words, about half the length of a standard contemporary novel and well under half of Tender Is the Night. It is also the only Fitzgerald novel most people can name, which is worth keeping in mind the next time somebody tells you a book has to run 90,000 words to count.

The Direct Answer
About 47,000 to 48,000 words.
The widely quoted 47,094 comes from user-submitted word count databases with no stated edition. A direct count of the 1925 Scribner text runs closer to 48,100. His biographer Matthew Bruccoli rounded it to 50,000 and used it as a unit of measure.

Why the Number Moves

47,094 is the figure that shows up on writing blogs, story structure sites, and half the threads about novel length. It originates on word count databases that accept user submissions and never say which edition was counted. That does not make it wrong. It does make it unverifiable, and four significant figures imply a precision nobody actually measured.

Counting the 1925 Scribner text directly, narrative only, gives about 48,100 words. That is roughly a thousand words above the database figure, a gap of about two percent, which is what different decisions about front matter and chapter headings will produce. Matthew Bruccoli, in his 1981 biography Some Sort of Epic Grandeur, twice refers to a Gatsby-length novel of 50,000 words as a standard unit.

Any of those three answers is defensible. Quoting 47,094 as though somebody counted it to the word is the only version that misleads.

How It Compares to His Other Novels

This Side of Paradise (1920) runs about 81,000 words. The Beautiful and Damned (1922) is his longest at roughly 124,000. Tender Is the Night (1934) comes in near 108,000 in its original arrangement, and The Last Tycoon stood at 44,000 words when he died in December 1940.

Fitzgerald planned Tender to the word. His 1932 general plan set it at a little over a hundred thousand words in fourteen chapters of 7,500 each, and the finished book landed near 108,000. He knew exactly how long a novel of his ran, which makes Gatsby's brevity a decision rather than an accident of pace.

Gatsby is the shortest completed novel he wrote and the one that lasted. The compression is why. There is no room in 47,000 words for a scene that does not carry weight.

The Number He Counted Instead

Fitzgerald kept a ledger from 1919 into the late 1930s, now held at the University of South Carolina. Its columns run to fees, agent commissions, net amounts, and an annual grand total, story by story, with the magazine and the date beside each entry. His grand total for 1929 came to $32,448.18. By 1931 it had reached $37,554.

The Saturday Evening Post was paying him $4,000 a story by 1929, up from $400 for his first sale in 1919. Those stories ran 5,000 to 6,000 words, which prices his prose near seventy cents a word at the peak. By 1939 he was writing 1,100 to 2,250-word short-shorts for Esquire at $250 apiece.

Over our garage is a large bare room whither I now retired with pencil, paper and oil stove, emerging the next afternoon at five o'clock with a 7,000-word story.

F. Scott Fitzgerald, How to Live on $36,000 a Year, Saturday Evening Post, 1924

The clearest daily word target anywhere in his record comes at the very end. On 14 December 1940 he drew up a schedule to finish the working draft of The Last Tycoon by 15 January: 1,750 words a day for the remaining 28,000. He died seven days later.

What You Can Steal

  • Short books can be the ones that last. Gatsby is 47,000 words. Nobody has ever complained that it feels slight.
  • Plan the length before you draft. Fitzgerald set Tender Is the Night at fourteen chapters of 7,500 words and delivered within a few thousand of the plan.
  • Know what a word of yours is worth. His ledger tracked earnings to the cent, which kept the fantasy out of a career built on freelance rates.
  • Set a daily number when the deadline is real. The one time Fitzgerald wrote down a words-per-day figure was when he had a manuscript to finish and a date to finish it by.
