How Many Words Is Ulysses?

Kia Orion | August 2, 2026 | 5 min read

Word counts for Ulysses get quoted with a confidence the evidence doesn't support. The book exists in several substantially different editions, the most famous of them the subject of a decades-long scholarly argument, so any single number is really a number about one printing.

The Direct Answer
Around 265,000 words, spread across 732 pages in the 1922 first edition.
No publisher or scholarly body has issued an official count. Figures in circulation run from about 264,000 to over 300,000 depending on the edition and the counting method, and the widely repeated claim of a 30,030-word vocabulary has no traceable original source. Read 265,000 as a good approximation.

Why the Number Moves

Sylvia Beach published Ulysses in Paris on 2 February 1922, Joyce's fortieth birthday, in an edition of 732 pages riddled with typesetting errors. French compositors were setting English they couldn't read from a manuscript covered in Joyce's insertions. Every edition since has been an argument about what the text should actually say, and Hans Walter Gabler's 1984 corrected edition introduced thousands of changes to the accepted reading.

Count a book whose text is contested and you get a contested number. The 265,000 figure circulates on reading-length sites and literary trivia pages without an attributed source, alongside a claim that Joyce used a vocabulary of 30,030 distinct words. Both figures are plausible and neither is traceable to a scholar who did the counting. Wikipedia's article on the novel lists a page count and declines to give a word count at all, which is the appropriate level of caution.

The count also answers the question most people are really asking, which is how long the book will take them. At an average adult reading pace of around 250 words a minute, 265,000 words is close to eighteen hours. Nobody reads Ulysses at that pace, because half of it resists being read at any pace, so double the estimate and you're closer to the truth.

For practical purposes, if someone asks how long Ulysses is, roughly a quarter of a million words is the honest answer. That makes it about three times the length of a standard novel and slightly longer than Order of the Phoenix, which the internet is happy to count precisely because nobody argues about the text of Harry Potter.

A Quarter Million Words in Seven Years

Joyce printed the timeline at the end of the book himself: Trieste, Zurich, Paris, 1914 to 1921. Seven years, through a world war and several household moves, with chapters serialized in The Little Review from 1918 until an obscenity prosecution shut it down in 1920.

Divide 265,000 words by seven years and you land somewhere near 100 words a day. That arithmetic is mine, not Joyce's, and it flattens a process that ran in bursts and stalls. It does square with the account his friend Frank Budgen gave of finding him exhausted after a full day's work with two sentences to show for it. The story is on how long Joyce took to write Ulysses, and it explains the arithmetic better than the arithmetic does.

He was also working with severely impaired eyesight for much of it, writing in thick crayon on large sheets of white cardboard so he could see what he'd put down. That's covered on how James Joyce wrote.

The Rest of the Shelf

Dubliners runs around 70,000 words across fifteen stories, with the closing story, The Dead, long enough at 15,000 to 16,000 words that it's often published on its own. A Portrait of the Artist as a Young Man is harder to pin down, with estimates ranging from about 75,000 to 96,000 depending on which aggregator you believe.

Finnegans Wake defeats counting almost by design. It took Joyce seventeen years, from 1923 to publication on 4 May 1939, and it's built from portmanteau words he invented, which makes any tally of distinct words a judgement call rather than a measurement. No authoritative count exists, and the figures floating around come from a single unverified source.

What You Can Steal

  • Big books are built in small units. Two sentences a day for seven years is 265,000 words. The arithmetic is more encouraging than it looks.
  • Serialize if you can. Publishing episodes of Ulysses in The Little Review kept the book alive and read while the whole thing was still years from done.
  • Set a deadline you actually care about. Joyce aimed publication at his own fortieth birthday and hit it.
  • Treat online word counts as estimates. Almost none of them cite a source, and for a text as unstable as Ulysses the number depends on which edition somebody fed into a script.
