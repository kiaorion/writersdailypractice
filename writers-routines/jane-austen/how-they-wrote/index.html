How Did Jane Austen Write?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Working in a shared room meant her method had to be portable and quiet. The system she built was small, hideable, and tuned to interruption.

The Direct Answer
On small sheets of paper she could put away or cover, keeping the work hidden from anyone outside the family.
Per A Memoir of Jane Austen (1869); the famous creaking-door detail comes from the same family account.

The Small-Sheet Method

Because she wrote where people gathered, she needed pages that could vanish in a second. Her nephew recorded the trick: "She wrote upon small sheets of paper which could easily be put away, or covered with a piece of blotting paper." A loose small sheet was easy to slide under something, and easy to hide entirely if a stranger appeared.

The privacy was aimed outward. The memoir says she was careful her occupation "should not be suspected by servants, or visitors, or any persons beyond her own family party." Inside the family the writing was no secret. They knew, and by Caroline's account they didn't disturb her at it.

She wrote upon small sheets of paper which could easily be put away, or covered with a piece of blotting paper.

James Edward Austen-Leigh, A Memoir of Jane Austen (1869)

The Creaking Door

The household's most quoted detail also comes from the memoir, so treat it as family recollection rather than something she wrote down herself. A swing door between the front of the house and the offices creaked when it opened. Austen refused to have it oiled, the memoir says, "because it gave her notice when anyone was coming." The creak bought her a few seconds to cover the page.

Whether the secrecy was as strict as later readers imagined is debated, since her family clearly knew and accepted the work. The mechanics, though, are consistent across the sources. To see how this fitted her day, read the full Jane Austen writing routine.

What You Can Steal

  • Make your work portable. Small pages meant she could write anywhere and clear it fast.
  • Build an early-warning system. A creaking door was her notification that focus time was ending.
  • Keep drafts private until you're ready. She shielded the work from outsiders while it was unfinished.
