Where Does J.K. Rowling Write?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Two places define the legend: a string of Edinburgh cafes where she drafted the first books with a baby asleep beside her, and a five-star hotel suite where she finished the last one. The cold-flat story you've heard is mostly wrong, and she's said so herself.

The Direct Answer
Edinburgh cafes for the early Potter books, the Balmoral Hotel for the finale.
She wrote longhand at Nicolson's and other cafes in the 1990s, then finished Deathly Hallows in room 552 of Edinburgh's Balmoral Hotel in January 2007.

The Edinburgh Cafes

After she moved to Edinburgh in the early 1990s, Rowling did much of her first-book writing in cafes, and the one she named most often was Nicolson's on South Bridge. Her brother-in-law had a stake in it, the staff let her linger, and she could nurse a single coffee for hours while her daughter Jessica slept in the pram. She was a single mother on a tight budget then, and a corner table she wouldn't be moved from was worth more than a quiet desk she didn't have.

I would go to Nicolson's cafe, because the staff were so nice and so patient there and allowed me to order one espresso and sit there for hours, writing until Jessica woke up.

J.K. Rowling, The Scotsman, 1997

She got a lot done in those snatched windows. In the same period she described how much writing was possible in a two-hour stretch when you knew it was the only chance you'd get that day. The constraint sharpened the work. There was no room to procrastinate when the clock was a sleeping baby.

The Elephant House on George IV Bridge gets the tourist plaque and the salvaged table, and she did write parts of later books there too. But the cafe writing wasn't a quaint aesthetic choice. Walking the baby in the pram was the reliable way to get her to nap, and once Jessica dropped off, a warm cafe was simply the nearest place to sit and work before she stirred again. Other Edinburgh spots, including the Traverse Theatre cafe, served the same purpose.

Why Not at Home? The Myth She Killed

The popular version says she wrote in cafes because she couldn't afford to heat her flat. Rowling has flatly denied it. In a 2001 BBC interview she said she wasn't daft enough to rent an unheated flat in an Edinburgh winter, and that her flat had heating. The cold-flat tale is a tidy bit of rags-to-riches myth-making that she's corrected more than once.

The real reasons were duller and more believable. A cafe gave her warmth, cheap coffee, background noise that was easier to work in than silence, and staff who didn't hover. Home meant a baby who slept badly and a thousand small chores pulling at her attention. The cafe was where the writing actually happened, so that's where she went.

The Balmoral Hotel

By the time she reached the seventh book, public cafe writing was impossible, so she booked herself into a suite at the Balmoral, the grand Victorian hotel above Waverley Station, to push through to the end without interruption. She finished Harry Potter and the Deathly Hallows there on 11 January 2007.

She marked the moment in a way that's now part of the building's folklore. On a marble bust of Hermes in the room, she wrote that she had finished the book in that room on that date. The suite has since been renamed after her, the bust sits protected under glass, and the room has become a small pilgrimage site for readers. It's a long way from one espresso at Nicolson's, but the method underneath was the same: find the space where the work can get finished, and stay there until it is.

What You Can Steal

  • Find a third place. A cafe, a library, anywhere that isn't home, can break the spell of distraction better than willpower.
  • Write in the gaps. She got chapters done in the two-hour windows a napping baby allowed.
  • Lean on a deadline space. When the stakes are high, a booked room with nothing else to do can carry you over the finish line.
  • Don't romanticize the struggle. The unheated-flat story is a myth. Comfortable and warm beats noble and frozen.
