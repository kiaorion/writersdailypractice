Kristin Hannah's Writing Advice

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Ask Kristin Hannah how to become a writer and the answer barely changes from interview to interview. Sit down, keep writing, and treat it as work rather than luck. She's blunt that talent alone won't carry anyone.

The Direct Answer
Keep writing no matter what, because discipline, more than talent, is what makes a writer.
On her official site FAQ she calls it the easiest answer to say and the hardest to actually do.

Sit Down and Keep Writing

When new writers ask Hannah how to break in, she gives an answer she admits is easy to say and hard to live: sit down and keep writing. The ones who make it, she says, keep writing no matter what. What separates them isn't a lucky break or a natural gift. It's the willingness to show up at the computer day after day to hone the craft, through the stretches when nothing seems to be landing. She's framed it as the most obvious advice in the world and also the most difficult to pull off.

The ones who make it keep writing no matter what.

Kristin Hannah, official site FAQ

Talent Isn't Enough

She's equally direct about the limits of talent. Don't let the world get you down and don't give up, she's told interviewers, and don't assume talent will carry you, because writing is hard work and discipline far more than luck. Her practical tips point the same way. Keep writing, take classes, make friends with other authors, and look at your own work critically. She's also said the story itself has to earn its keep, that the job is to write a powerful, emotional story that keeps a reader turning pages, whatever the book happens to be about.

Her own path backs the point up. Hannah practiced law before she wrote full time, and she returned to fiction while pregnant and on bed rest, then gave herself years to break in before her first novel sold. The advice isn't theory. It's how she got here.

That advice mirrors how she actually works. See the full Kristin Hannah writing routine.

What You Can Steal

  • Keep writing no matter what. Hannah's whole message is persistence over talent or luck.
  • Show up daily. She credits the writers who sit down at the computer day after day, good days and bad.
  • Don't lean on talent. She's clear that hard work and discipline do the real work.
  • Aim for emotion. Write a story that keeps a reader turning pages, whatever it's about.
