What Is Madeline Miller's Writing Routine?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Madeline Miller doesn't write in marathon bursts anymore. These days she keeps to a morning block, leans hard on walking, and likes the house dark and quiet. Here's what a working day looks like for her.

The Direct Answer
She writes from mid-morning until early afternoon, then moves her body to shake something loose.
Miller starts around 8:30 to 9 a.m. and works until lunch or early afternoon, when, as she told PBS NewsHour, her inspiration dries up.

A Morning Block, Then a Walk

Miller gets going in the morning, somewhere around 8:30 or 9, and writes until early afternoon. That's roughly when the well runs dry and frustration sets in, so she stops pushing the page and gets up. For Miller, exercise is part of the writing itself. Movement helps the work along.

A walk or a workout is her go-to fix whenever she's stuck. She's said movement is completely vital to her process, and that a walk is her surefire remedy for creative stagnation. No matter how stalled she feels, she tends to work the problem out on foot.

Writing for me is like descending to the bottom of the ocean. It takes me a long time going down, and a long time returning to air, and I have significant fallow periods in between.

Madeline Miller, PBS NewsHour

Dark Rooms and Method Writing

Miller writes a bit like an actor getting into character. She's described pulling a hood over her head in a darkened room to block out stimuli, so she can put on the character and see through their eyes. When she's deep in a book, she likes the story to be the last thing on her mind before she falls asleep.

The quiet, dark house does real work. It lets her drop into the world of the novel without the day pulling at her. For the fuller picture, see the full Madeline Miller writing routine.

How the Routine Changed

It wasn't always this steady. While she wrote The Song of Achilles, Miller was teaching full time, so the book got written in twelve and fourteen hour binges on weekends and school breaks. With young children now, those all-day marathons aren't realistic, so she's traded them for regular daily hours she can actually keep.

What You Can Steal

  • Protect a morning block. Write while the tank is full, stop when it's empty.
  • Walk when you're stuck. Miller solves plot problems on her feet, not at the desk.
  • Set the room to match the work. Dark and quiet helps her disappear into a scene.
  • Trade binges for a pace you can repeat. Steady daily hours beat heroics you can't sustain.
