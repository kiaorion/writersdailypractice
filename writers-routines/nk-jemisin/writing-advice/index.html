N.K. Jemisin's Writing Advice

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Jemisin wrote for years before her breakout, so her advice leans practical rather than mystical. It comes down to staying in the game, doing the homework, and building characters readers can't look away from. Here's the advice she returns to most.

The Direct Answer
Keep going, do the real research, and give every main character a life beyond the plot.
Her core message is persistence plus craft, hard-earned across years of submitting before The Fifth Season broke through.

Keep Going, and Ignore the Naysayers

The advice Jemisin repeats most is also the least glamorous. Keep going. She frames it as continuing to work on your craft, continuing to improve, and continuing to submit until you break through. Her own path backs it up. She wrote for years before the Broken Earth trilogy made her the first author to win the Hugo for Best Novel three years running, once for each book in a single trilogy. She's also been open about ignoring people who doubted that traditional publishing would make room for a Black writer in science fiction and fantasy, and going for it anyway.

Persistence, for her, includes protecting the person doing the writing. She's spoken candidly about depression and about writers putting their own health first, since there's no career to build if you burn out the human behind it. She also sends writers toward each other. She's said artists need real contact with other people to make good work, and that a critique group teaches you as much by watching others get critiqued as by taking notes on your own pages.

Write Characters Worth Following

Jemisin is blunt about research. If you're putting real people and cultures into a story, study them instead of using them as props or set dressing, and if your world leans on a real field, learn enough of it to be honest. She treats that study as part of the job of writing, not an optional flourish you can skip.

She also pushes back on the usual rulebook for magic. Yes, a magic system needs internal logic and consistent rules, but she warns writers against letting the magical turn workaday. The wonder is a big part of why readers show up, so the extraordinary should still land as extraordinary on the page instead of dissolving into a system of mechanics.

On process, she's relaxed about outlining. She tends to work out where a book begins and where it ends, then lets the path between them develop as she writes rather than mapping every turn in advance. That works because she's willing to revise, treating a rough early draft as raw material to shape instead of a verdict on whether she can pull the book off.

On the page, her advice circles back to character. She works to give every main character a rich inner life, with families, beliefs, motivations, and quirks that exist outside the plot. Then she puts them under pressure. Fiction, in her view, has to make the protagonist uncomfortable, in danger, or grieving, because that discomfort is what makes a story worth reading. Comfortable characters are dull ones. See the full N.K. Jemisin writing routine for how these ideas show up in her daily practice.

What You Can Steal

  • Persist and keep submitting. She wrote for years before three straight Hugos.
  • Do the real research. Study people and fields instead of using them as props.
  • Join a critique group. You often learn most watching others get edited.
  • Give characters a life outside the plot, then make them uncomfortable.
