How Long Is The Name of the Wind? Rothfuss by the Numbers

Kia Orion | August 2, 2026 | 5 min read

Rothfuss is the rare case where the word count is the story. Two enormous books published four years apart, then fifteen years of quiet on the third. The numbers explain his method better than any interview does.

The Direct Answer
Commonly cited at around 250,000 words. The sequel runs closer to 400,000.
Neither Rothfuss nor DAW Books has published an official figure. The hardcovers run 662 and 994 pages, which puts the two published volumes together somewhere near two thirds of a million words. He has never worked to a daily quota.

What the Two Books Actually Weigh

The Name of the Wind ran 662 pages in the DAW hardcover when it appeared on 27 March 2007. The Wise Man's Fear ran 994 pages on 1 March 2011. Neither Rothfuss nor his publisher has released an official word count, so the figures that circulate, around 250,000 for the first book and close to 400,000 for the second, are estimates worked back from page counts and typesetting.

Treat them as approximations with a wide margin. What is not in doubt is the scale. The Wise Man's Fear is one of the longest single volumes in modern commercial fantasy, and the two published books together run to something near two thirds of a million words.

For a sense of that: it is more words than War and Peace, delivered inside four years. Then the tap closed.

There Is No Daily Number

Rothfuss has been consistent across blog posts and convention panels that he does not work to a word quota and does not produce at a predictable rate. The books come out of a long cycle of drafting and rewriting rather than a daily accumulation, and he has said the slowness is a feature of how he works rather than a temporary problem.

That is the opposite of the model most working novelists use, and it is worth understanding why the difference matters. A quota writer with a bad month loses a month. A revision writer with a bad year can lose the year and have nothing external to show for it, because all of the work happened inside a draft nobody else sees.

The Fifteen-Year Number

The Wise Man's Fear was published in March 2011. As of 2026, The Doors of Stone has not appeared. That is fifteen years between volumes two and three of a trilogy that began life as one long manuscript, written over roughly seven years before the first book ever sold.

The situation became public in July 2020 when his editor, Betsy Wollheim, wrote on Facebook that she had never seen a word of book three. In December 2021 Rothfuss promised a full chapter if his Worldbuilders charity drive reached $333,333. The drive hit the number. The chapter has not been released, and in April 2022 he said the work was moving more slowly than he would like.

None of that is a moral failure, and it is not unusual in kind, only in degree. It is the clearest available argument for the thing quota writers say about quotas. A daily number is a floor. Take the floor away and the only measure left is the finished book, and finished books can take a very long time to arrive.

What You Can Steal

  • A quota is a floor, not a cage. Rothfuss's method produced two of the most admired fantasy novels of the century and a fifteen-year gap. The floor is what prevents the gap.
  • Long books need a length target early. A 994-page second volume is a structural decision, and it is easier to make on purpose than to discover in draft eleven.
  • Revision without a stopping rule does not stop. Decide in advance what finished looks like, in pages or in passes.
  • Splitting one manuscript into a trilogy creates a debt. The Kingkiller books were drafted as a single long manuscript and sold as three. That works beautifully until the last third needs finishing.
