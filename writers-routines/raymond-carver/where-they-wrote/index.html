Where Did Raymond Carver Write?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

For most of his early career, Carver had no study, no desk of his own, sometimes no quiet room at all. So he wrote wherever he could steal an hour, and the most famous of those places was his own parked car.

The Direct Answer
Wherever he could steal an hour, most famously in his parked car and during his night shifts as a hospital janitor, because for years he had no room of his own.
He told The Paris Review he'd sit in the car with a pad on his knee just to get a few lines down.

The Car

The best-documented answer is the oddest one. With two kids, a rented house, and no space to himself, Carver would go out to the parked car to write. In his 1983 Paris Review interview he described it plainly.

I used to go out and sit in the car and try to write something on a pad on my knee.

Raymond Carver, The Paris Review, 1983

He framed it as sheer frustration, wanting to write and never finding the time or the place. There was nothing romantic about it. The car was the one private space he had.

The Night Shift, and No Studio for Years

In the mid-1960s in Sacramento, Carver worked as a night custodian at Mercy Hospital. By his account he'd finish all the actual cleaning in the first hour, then spend the rest of the shift writing. The job quietly funded a nightly writing residency.

The rest of the time he wrote in whatever corner was free, wedged between jobs and childcare. A proper room of his own came late, after the stories found readers. For the day-to-day picture, see the full Raymond Carver writing routine.

What You Can Steal

  • Any door that shuts will do. Carver's was a car. A parked car still works.
  • Steal time from the day job. Finish the required work fast, then write.
  • Stop waiting for the perfect room. He wrote his best early stories without one.
  • Keep a pad within reach. His sat on his knee.
