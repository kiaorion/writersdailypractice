What Is Sarah J. Maas's Writing Schedule?

Kia Orion | June 28, 2026 | 5 min read

Readers who imagine every bestselling novelist at a desk by 6 a.m. with a fixed word count will be surprised by Sarah J. Maas. She's talked about her process across more than a decade of interviews, and none of it looks like a rigid daily schedule. It looks closer to sprints, with long, quiet stretches between them where the writing isn't visibly happening at all.

The Direct Answer
She works in binge-drafting sprints, not a fixed daily schedule.
Maas has described going deep on a manuscript for concentrated stretches rather than writing a set number of words every single day, fitting the work around family life, book tours, and deadlines instead of a fixed morning routine.

No Publicized Daily Word Count

Unlike novelists who've built a public persona around a specific daily quota, Maas hasn't attached herself to a number. She hasn't gone on record with something like a fixed count of words every morning before breakfast. What she has talked about is intensity: when she's in a drafting phase, she goes hard, and when she isn't, she doesn't force pages out of habit.

Binge-Drafting in Sprints

The term that comes up most often in how Maas describes her own process is binge-drafting. Rather than a steady daily trickle, she'll go deep on a manuscript for an extended stretch, staying inside the book's world for days or weeks at a time. For a writer managing three interlocking fantasy series with dense continuity, staying immersed for a long sprint likely cuts down on the time spent re-orienting to where the story left off.

That kind of schedule requires blocking out real chunks of time rather than protecting a single morning hour, which means her calendar probably looks less like a routine and more like a series of planned drafting windows built around everything else in her life.

Writing Around Motherhood and a Public Career

Maas is a mother, and she's spoken about balancing her writing career with family life rather than treating the two as separate tracks. She's also a highly visible author with a large fan community, book tours, and a busy publication schedule spanning multiple ongoing series. All of that competes for the same hours a purely private novelist wouldn't have to negotiate. Her schedule, by necessity, bends around those demands rather than the other way around.

The Playlist as a Ritual, Not the Clock

If there's a consistent ritual in her process, it isn't a wake-up time. It's the playlist. Maas builds a distinct set of songs for each book before or during drafting, and she's described using it to hold a story's emotional tone steady across a long writing period. It works as her way back into a manuscript's headspace each time she sits down, which matters more when sessions are spread unevenly across weeks than when you're writing the same hour every day.

What You Can Steal

  • If a fixed daily quota makes you anxious, try scheduling drafting sprints instead. Block out several concentrated days rather than forcing an hour into an already full one.
  • Build a ritual that travels with you across an inconsistent schedule. A playlist works well because it doesn't require a specific room or time of day.
  • Stop comparing your schedule to writers who keep a fixed morning routine. Plenty of working novelists write around their lives instead of building their lives around writing.
  • Protect the sprint when it comes. If your creative energy runs in bursts, treat the burst as non-negotiable time, even if the days around it look empty.
