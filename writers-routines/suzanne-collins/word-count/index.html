How Many Words Is The Hunger Games?

Kia Orion | August 2, 2026 | 5 min read

The Hunger Games runs to roughly 100,000 words. So does Catching Fire, and so does Mockingjay. The three books sit within about two thousand words of each other, which almost never happens by chance across a trilogy written over three years.

The Direct Answer
About 100,000 words each, for all three trilogy books.
Accelerated Reader counts give The Hunger Games 99,750 words, Catching Fire 101,564, and Mockingjay 100,269. A 2013 Slate textual analysis put all three slightly higher and inside the same narrow band. Call it 100,000 and you will not be far off any of them.

The Trilogy, Counted Two Ways

Two independent counts exist and they disagree politely. Renaissance Learning's Accelerated Reader database, which counts the text of a specific edition to assign reading levels, gives 99,750 for The Hunger Games, 101,564 for Catching Fire, and 100,269 for Mockingjay. Ben Blatt's textual analysis for Slate in 2013 came in at roughly 101,200, 101,900, and 102,700.

The gap between the two sets is a tokenizer argument rather than a real disagreement. Both find the same thing, which is a trilogy of nearly identical length, and Blatt made that consistency the point of his piece. Across three books written under enormous commercial pressure after the first one broke out, Collins landed each volume within about 1,500 words of the others.

In print the US hardcovers run 374, 391, and 390 pages. Page counts drift more than word counts do, since trim size and type settings change between printings, so the word figures make the better comparison.

The Prequels Broke the Pattern

The Ballad of Songbirds and Snakes arrived in 2020 at about 150,442 words across 517 pages, half again as long as anything in the original trilogy. Sunrise on the Reaping followed in 2025 at roughly 112,440 words and 400 pages. Both are Capitol-side stories carrying more political machinery, which is a plausible reason for the extra room.

Add the five Underland Chronicles books, which run between about 55,000 and 79,000 words each, and Collins's ten novels come to roughly 880,000 words published between 2003 and 2025. Averaged across those twenty-two years that is about 40,000 words a year. Brandon Sanderson does that in about seven weeks.

The comparison is not a criticism. Collins publishes when she has an argument to make and stays quiet otherwise, including the ten-year silence between Mockingjay in 2010 and the first prequel in 2020. She has never explained that gap publicly, and anything you read claiming to know the reason is guessing.

Why the Counts Are So Even

Collins came to novels from television, where an act has to end at a fixed point because a commercial is coming. She has said she works out the key structural points before she starts writing: the inciting incident, the act breaks, the mid-story reversal, the crisis, the climax. She began as a playwright more than forty years ago and has used the same dramatic template for every book since.

A writer who knows where the act breaks fall before the first sentence exists will produce books of similar length without aiming at it. The structure sets the size. Word count becomes an output of the plan instead of a target to chase.

She has never published a daily word count, and it would be out of character if she did. The one output figure she has given is time-based: three to five hours of actual writing makes a productive day, and a day spent untangling a plot problem counts too.

What You Can Steal

  • Decide the structure before you draft and the length takes care of itself. Three Hunger Games books within 1,500 words of each other is what planning looks like from the outside.
  • Use the act break as your unit. Collins builds in television acts, which is why her chapters end at the exact point where stopping feels wrong.
  • A consistent book length is a repeatable book. Once you know your novel runs 100,000 words across a fixed set of acts, the next one has a shape before you write a word.
  • Silence is allowed. Ten years between books cost Collins nothing. Publishing on a schedule you do not believe in costs more.
