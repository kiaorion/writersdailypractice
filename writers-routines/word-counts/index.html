How Many Words a Day Do Famous Authors Write?

Every figure below is sourced on the page it links to. Where an author never kept a daily count, this says so instead of guessing, because a number invented for the sake of the table is worth less than an honest blank.

Common questions

How many words a day should I write?

There is no single right number. Among authors who kept a documented count, the range runs from Garcia Marquez at four or five lines a day to Kerouac at roughly 6,000 during a three week sprint. The most common figures cluster between 500 and 2,000. Hemingway averaged about 500, Stephen King aims for 2,000 with a floor of 1,000, and Graham Greene famously worked to 500.

Which famous author wrote the most words per day?

Of the authors with a documented figure, Jack Kerouac's roughly 6,000 words a day during the April 1951 scroll draft is the highest sustained rate, though it lasted three weeks rather than a career. Isaac Asimov worked at 4,000 to 5,000 on a normal day across decades, which is the more remarkable long-run number.

Did every famous author track a daily word count?

No, and many of the best did not. Toni Morrison never set a target and said so directly. Ursula K. Le Guin measured the morning rather than the output. Joan Didion retyped from page one every day, which makes a daily count close to meaningless. Counting words is one method among several, not the standard.
