Writers' Routines

The Daily Writing Routines of 35+ Famous Authors, Compared

How much did they write a day? When did they work? How did they draft? Here is the whole shelf side by side, pulled from interviews, memoirs, and their own words. Every row links to the full routine. Steal what fits.

AuthorWords a dayWhen they wroteHow they wroteWhere
Maya Angelou·She left home at six and was at work by 6:30 a.m.·In a rented hotel room, stripped bare, lying across the bed.
Margaret AtwoodBetween roughly 1,000 and 2,000 words on a productive day.Mid-morning into the afternoon, usually wrapping around 4pm.··
Jane Austen·In the mornings at Chawton, fitted around breakfast and the running of the house.On small sheets of paper she could put away or cover, keeping the work hidden from anyone outside the family.At a small twelve-sided walnut table in the family sitting room at Chawton Cottage.
James Baldwin·At night, until about three or four in the morning.·In a stone house in Saint-Paul-de-Vence, in the south of France.
Ray BradburyAt least 1,000 words a day, nearly every day.··In the basement typing room of UCLA's Powell Library.
Octavia Butler·Before dawn, often rising somewhere between 2 and 4 a.m.Through routine, self-written contracts, and notebooks of affirmations.·
Agatha Christie·She had no fixed routine and wrote in spells and bursts.Mostly in her head, away from the desk, before a word was typed.Almost anywhere, with no dedicated room or fixed desk.
Charles Dickens·He wrote from roughly 9am to 2pm, then walked a dozen or more miles.With a goose-quill and blue ink, at a desk arranged in a fixed order.In a quiet study at a desk set before a window.
Joan Didion·Her day was built around one hour alone before dinner.She typed her work, drafts and revisions alike.·
Fyodor Dostoevsky·He wrote at night, alone, from late evening into the early hours of the morning.He planned obsessively in notebooks, then drafted fast, sometimes by dictation, and revised hard.·
F. Scott Fitzgerald··In intense bursts, followed by obsessive revision.Mostly at Villa Marie in Valescure, near Saint-Raphael, France.
John Grisham·He woke at 5am and wrote his first word by 5:30.He wrote one page a day for about three years around a full-time job.·
Ursula K. Le Guin·She wrote every morning, roughly 7:15 until noon, after a slow 5:30 start.She wrote in the morning, by ear, then revised the draft heavily.·
Ernest HemingwayAbout 500 words per day, on average.Every morning, starting at first light (around 6 a.m.) and finishing by midday.·Yes. Hemingway wrote standing up, at a chest-high board, for most of his career.
James Joyce·About seven years, from roughly 1914 to 1921, with publication on February 2, 1922.Joyce usually wrote lying on his stomach in bed, in a white coat, using large blue pencils, all to cope with failing eyeAcross three cities: Trieste, Zurich, and Paris, over the years 1914 to 1921.
Franz Kafka·Kafka wrote late at night, usually from about 11pm onward.Kafka wrote in intense nighttime bursts, fitted around a full-time office job.Kafka wrote at home, in his room inside his family's crowded Prague apartment.
Jack Kerouac·He wrote at night in fast, unedited bursts, with a candle ritual to bracket each session.He typed the first draft in about three weeks on one continuous 120-foot scroll.·
Stephen King2,000 words per day (goal). 1,000 words per day (floor).In the morning, sitting down between 8:00 and 8:30.First draft with the door closed, then revision with the door open.·
Gabriel Garcia Marquez·Every morning, about nine to two or three, after taking his kids to school.In familiar, warmed-up surroundings, on his own machine, never a borrowed one.·
George R.R. Martin··An old DOS computer running WordStar 4.0.In Santa Fe, in an office across the street from his home.
Cormac McCarthy·He worked in near-total isolation and kept his overhead low so almost nothing competed with the writing.A light-blue Olivetti Lettera 32 manual portable typewriter.·
Toni Morrison·Before dawn, usually starting between 4 and 5am.In pre-dawn hours and stolen pockets of time, around a day job and two children.·
Haruki MurakamiAbout ten manuscript pages, roughly 1,600 words a day.Early morning, starting around 4 a.m.··
Flannery O&#x27;Connor·She wrote every morning from about 9 a.m. to noon, after Mass.·At Andalusia, her Georgia farm, at a desk facing the back of a wardrobe.
Joyce Carol Oates·In the morning, often before breakfast.She drafts in longhand first, then types.·
George Orwell··For four reasons: egoism, aesthetics, history, and politics.At Barnhill, a remote farmhouse on the island of Jura.
Sylvia Plath·At about four in the morning, before her two children woke.In a concentrated predawn burst in the autumn of 1962, often a poem a day.·
Marcel Proust·He lived by night. Proust slept through the day and wrote in bed until dawn.In bed, by hand, in notebooks, expanding the manuscript endlessly with pinned-on paper add-ons.To shut out the noise, dust, and light that triggered his asthma and shattered his focus.
J.K. Rowling··She writes by hand first, especially drafts and plotting, then types it up.Edinburgh cafes for the early Potter books, the Balmoral Hotel for the finale.
John Steinbeck·Roughly a page a day, in pencil, after a written warm-up.Yes. He wrote longhand in pencil, often a dozen or more a day.·
J.R.R. Tolkien··Slowly and by hand over roughly twelve years, mostly late at night, with the invented languages built first.In his cluttered home study in North Oxford, with the Inklings hearing it read aloud at the Eagle and Child pub.
Leo Tolstoy·A protected block from mid-morning through the afternoon, alone in his study, undisturbed.Over roughly six years, in a cycle of drafting and obsessive rewriting, with his wife Sophia copying the manuscript out At Yasnaya Polyana, his family estate south of Moscow, in a modest study he used for nearly all his major work.
Mark Twain·He wrote from after breakfast until dinner around five, skipped lunch, and read the day's work aloud at night.He wrote longhand for most of his life, then dictated from bed in his final years.In a small octagonal study on a hill at Quarry Farm, near Elmira, New York.
Kurt Vonnegut·Up at 5:30, writing by 8, a midday swim, asleep by ten.One sentence at a time, perfected before moving on.·
Virginia Woolf·In the morning, roughly from after ten o'clock until lunch.By hand first, in pen and ink. The typewriter came later, for revision.In a writing lodge at the bottom of the garden at Monk's House, Rodmell, Sussex.
